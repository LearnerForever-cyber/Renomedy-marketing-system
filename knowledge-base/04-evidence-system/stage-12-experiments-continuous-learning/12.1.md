# 12.1 — Experiment Design and Pre-registration

**Status:** Proposed experiment standard — owner approval pending

## Experiment brief
Record experiment ID, date, owner, decision to inform, hypothesis, rationale/source, audience, intervention, comparison if appropriate, primary outcome, guardrail outcomes, method, recruitment/sample plan, duration/stopping rule, success and failure criteria, ethical/privacy review, risks, dependencies, and analysis plan.

## Design rules
- State what result would disconfirm the hypothesis.
- Choose a metric that matches the decision rather than the easiest available number.
- Define success criteria before collecting results.
- Record assumptions and likely confounders.
- Use a control/comparison only when appropriate and ethical.
- Avoid collecting sensitive health information unless essential and covered by an approved process.
- Do not run an experiment that misleads people about product availability, medical capability, or data handling.

## Concept-stage experiment ideas (not yet run)
- Compare two accurate concept descriptions for comprehension, not only click appeal.
- Test whether people can distinguish future vision from currently available functionality.
- Explore which coordination tasks participants recall without prompting.
- Test whether a proposed research invitation is understandable and has a functioning destination.

Each idea requires owner approval, an appropriate protocol, and predefined criteria before execution.

## Stopping rules
Stop if the experiment creates confusion about medical advice, collects data unexpectedly, misrepresents availability, causes undue pressure, or reveals a safety/privacy issue. Record the reason and do not discard adverse findings.
