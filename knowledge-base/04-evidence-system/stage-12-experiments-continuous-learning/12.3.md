# 12.3 — Continuous Improvement and Knowledge-Base Updates

**Status:** Proposed learning loop — owner approval pending

## Learning loop
1. Identify a decision or uncertainty.
2. Write a falsifiable hypothesis and predeclare the evidence threshold.
3. Choose a proportionate, ethical test.
4. Collect and record evidence with provenance.
5. Analyze results, limitations, and contradictory observations.
6. Make a human-approved decision.
7. Update the source register, fact ledger, open-question backlog, and affected stage documents.
8. Record what changed, why, and what remains unknown.
9. Revisit the outcome when new evidence or changed conditions justify it.

## Change control
When editing a stage document, preserve prior meaningful decisions and mark superseded statements clearly. Update related links and stage README indexes. A material status change should be reflected consistently in the master README, relevant block README, product truth, fact ledger, source register, and campaign guidance.

## Agent contribution limits
Future agents may summarize evidence, identify contradictions, draft experiment plans, and propose updates. They must not fabricate research, promote a hypothesis to fact without evidence, label an experiment successful without applying its predeclared criteria, or approve sensitive claims autonomously.

## Learning quality checks
- What did we actually observe?
- What alternative explanation could fit the data?
- What evidence contradicts our preferred story?
- What is still unknown?
- Which decision changes because of this learning?
- Which records and public claims need to be updated?

## Completion criteria
The learning loop is operational only after the owner approves the process, assigns accountable people, and at least one real experiment is documented honestly from brief through decision. Until then, this stage is a proposed operating standard, not evidence of an active experimentation program.
