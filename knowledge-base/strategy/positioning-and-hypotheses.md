# Positioning and Validation Hypotheses

## Public-facing positioning supported by the current website

Renomedy is exploring a shared place for families to organize everyday medicine-routine details and keep relevant family context together, with a focus on clarity and coordination.

The current public headline is “Care works better, together.” The website clearly labels the concept as in development and not yet available.

## Internal long-term positioning hypothesis

The owner-provided project context describes a broader ambition to connect prescription understanding, medication organization, dose-event records, continuity awareness and family coordination.

This broader positioning must not be presented as the currently available product.

## Differentiation hypothesis

A family-oriented workflow that brings routine details and context together may be more useful than scattered notes and conversations. This must be validated with users and should not be framed as a proven competitive advantage.

## Customer value hypotheses

1. Clarity: families may value having everyday routine details easier to organize.
2. Coordination: family members may value shared context.
3. Respectful support: caregivers may value coordination that does not remove the medicine user's agency.
4. Future continuity: the broader product vision may support more connected medication-management workflows if implemented safely.

## Validation rules

- Likes, views and clicks are attention signals, not proof of product value.
- Sign-ups or expressions of interest are intent signals, not proof of sustained use.
- Do not create fake reviews, testimonials, customer counts, statistics or success stories.
- Record the audience, problem, message, CTA and observed result for each experiment.
- Separate evidence from assumptions in campaign reports.
