# Priority Audiences

## Audiences explicitly named on the public website

The site has sections for:
- Parents.
- Adult children.
- Family caregivers.

It describes adult children who may live apart and family caregiving that may involve more than one person. This establishes the intended audience direction, not validated market demand.

## Internal audience context

The owner-provided project context also identifies people managing chronic conditions, older adults and households managing multiple medicines as potential user groups. These are internal audience definitions, not evidence that these groups have been validated or acquired.

## Primary working hypothesis

Adult children helping their parents and family caregivers may be a useful initial audience for testing whether a shared, organized view of everyday medicine-routine details solves a meaningful problem.

This is a hypothesis to validate, not a proven segment.

## Research hypotheses to test

- Family routine details can be scattered across conversations, notes and memory.
- Multiple family members may need a shared understanding of routine information.
- Caregivers may value clarity without taking away an older adult's independence.
- People may be cautious about sharing health-related details or relying on AI.

## Respectful representation

- Show older adults as people with agency and preferences.
- Avoid suggesting all older adults are dependent or forgetful.
- Recognize caregiver effort without romanticizing exhaustion or inducing guilt.
- Do not invent an individual's medical history or suggest every family faces the same problem.

## Validation questions

- Who experiences the problem most acutely: the medicine user, caregiver, or both?
- Which routine details are genuinely difficult to coordinate?
- What do families use today?
- What would make a family trust or distrust this concept?
- Which information would they not want to share?
- What evidence would make someone willing to try the eventual product?
