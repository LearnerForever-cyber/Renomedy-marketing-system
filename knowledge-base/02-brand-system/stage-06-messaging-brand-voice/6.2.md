# 6.2 — Voice, Tone and Language Rules

**Status:** Proposed voice rules — owner approval pending

## Recommended voice
- **Clear:** everyday words before technical terms.
- **Calm:** no alarmism around health or caregiving.
- **Respectful:** speak about older adults and caregivers as people with agency.
- **Honest:** distinguish what exists from what is being explored.
- **Practical:** describe the task or concept without promising a medical result.
- **Warm but not sentimental:** do not use guilt, fear, or exaggerated intimacy to motivate action.

These are recommendations for owner review, not independently verified audience preferences.

## Language rules
- Prefer “medicine-routine details” or “family coordination” where accurate and understandable.
- Use “concept,” “exploring,” and “in development” when discussing future functionality.
- Avoid “ensures,” “guarantees,” “clinically proven,” “perfect accuracy,” “never miss a dose,” and similar absolutes unless a precise, supportable use exists; for medical outcome guarantees, do not use them.
- Do not call Renomedy a medical professional, clinical decision-maker, or replacement for professional care.
- Explain uncertainty instead of hiding it in fine print.

## Regional language
The first language and region priorities are not confirmed in the reviewed sources. Do not assume that a single English phrase translates safely or naturally across Indian languages. Localized health-related copy should be reviewed by competent speakers and appropriate subject-matter reviewers.

## Voice QA
Check each draft for factual support, clarity, dignity, pressure tactics, ambiguity about availability, and whether a reasonable reader might infer a medical promise.
