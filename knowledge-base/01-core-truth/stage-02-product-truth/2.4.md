# 2.4 — AI, OCR & Automation Claims

**Status:** Working draft — owner approval pending

## What is documented
The owner-provided Renomedy Context (S2) describes AI-assisted prescription understanding as part of the intended product vision. It records ML Kit, Tesseract, MedGemma, Groq, and Gemini as technologies that have been explored, while explicitly noting that the exact current pipeline and production configuration are not established.

The Fact Ledger classifies AI-assisted prescription understanding as owner context / internal planning only, not a live feature claim.

**Evidence label:** Internal record; not independently verified as an implementation.  
**Sources:** [Fact Ledger](../../sources/fact-ledger.md), [Source Register](../../sources/source-register.md), owner-provided Renomedy Context (S2).

## Do not overstate the technology
The following statements are **not established by the reviewed evidence**:
- that a specific OCR engine or AI model is currently integrated;
- that a particular model is used in production;
- that a complete prescription-image processing pipeline exists;
- that OCR output is medically accurate or reliable enough for consequential use;
- that the system resolves handwriting ambiguity safely;
- that a human verification step exists in the current product;
- that any model has been clinically validated for Renomedy's intended workflow.

Mentioning a technology in planning notes proves only that it was discussed or explored—not that it is integrated, deployed, licensed for the use, or performing successfully.

## Explain OCR and AI carefully
OCR (optical character recognition) attempts to convert visible text in an image into machine-readable text. Extracted text can be incomplete or wrong. AI-generated interpretation can introduce additional errors or unwarranted confidence.

For prescription-related information, a readable extraction is not automatically a correct interpretation, and a correct-looking interpretation is not automatically an approved instruction for a patient to follow.

## Safe public phrasing while status remains unverified
Prefer:
- “Renomedy is exploring ways to make medicine-routine information easier for families to organize.”
- “AI-assisted prescription understanding is part of the longer-term product vision.” (Only when discussing the vision, not current functionality.)
- “The product is in development; specific capabilities and availability have not been confirmed.”

Avoid:
- “Scan any prescription instantly.”
- “Reads every handwritten prescription accurately.”
- “AI verifies your medication.”
- “Automatically creates a safe prescription schedule.”
- “Never misreads a medicine.”
- “Clinically accurate AI” or “clinically proven” without specific, applicable evidence and approval.
- Naming a model/provider as a current product dependency without current technical evidence and owner approval.

## Evidence required before any stronger claim
Before stating that AI/OCR functionality exists or performs to a given level, obtain:
1. current demonstration or release evidence showing the precise workflow;
2. confirmation of the actual deployed components and operating context;
3. evaluation data relevant to the intended inputs, languages, handwriting, and failure modes;
4. documented uncertainty handling and human confirmation for consequential ambiguity;
5. appropriate professional, safety, privacy, and legal review;
6. approved wording that reflects limitations rather than implying certainty.

Do not invent accuracy percentages, evaluation sample sizes, benchmarks, or validation results.

## Open questions
- Has any prescription OCR or AI workflow progressed beyond exploration?
- If a prototype exists, what exactly does it do, and in which environment?
- What test evidence exists, and what inputs are excluded?
- How are uncertain, missing, or conflicting fields handled?
- What safeguards prevent an extracted or generated result from being mistaken for a clinical instruction?
