# 1.4 — Foundational Principles & Boundaries

**Status:** Proposed principles — owner approval pending  
These are proposed operating rules and should be approved or edited before being treated as final policy.

## 1. Trust before growth
Do not pursue attention, conversions, or engagement with unsupported, alarming, manipulative, or unclear claims. A smaller, well-informed audience is preferable to growth built on misunderstanding.

## 2. People remain in control of health decisions
Renomedy is not a doctor or pharmacist. Content and product concepts must not tell an individual to start, stop, skip, combine, or change medication. Individual treatment questions belong with a qualified healthcare professional.

## 3. Product truth is non-negotiable
A feature mentioned in a plan, design, prototype, codebase, or internal discussion is not automatically live. Public claims require evidence of current behavior and appropriate approval. Current public status remains “in development / not yet available” unless updated with verified evidence.

## 4. Evidence before certainty
Distinguish observed facts, source-backed statements, owner context, hypotheses, and unknowns. Record sources and review dates. When evidence is incomplete, state that plainly.

## 5. Respect the family context
Family coordination can involve sensitive relationships, uneven responsibilities, and different preferences. Do not assume that one relative should automatically access another person's health information. Consent, access, privacy, and individual autonomy require explicit consideration.

## 6. Reduce friction without adding burden
A proposed feature or campaign should make a real task easier to understand or complete. Do not add complexity merely because it is technically possible or makes the product sound advanced.

## 7. Design for comprehension and accessibility
Use plain language, clear hierarchy, legible content, and respectful visuals. Avoid shame, stereotypes, ageist assumptions, and language that portrays people as incapable of managing their own care.

## 8. Learn from behavior, not applause
Positive comments, survey intent, clicks, and interview enthusiasm do not by themselves prove adoption, retention, willingness to pay, or outcomes. Define what each metric can and cannot establish.

## 9. Human review for consequential claims
AI-generated content and automated review are aids, not final authority. Health claims, product availability, privacy/security statements, regulatory language, and sensitive testimonials require appropriate human verification and approval.

## 10. Protect sensitive information by default
Collect only what is necessary for a defined purpose. Do not invite people to post personal medical details publicly. Research and marketing workflows should avoid unnecessary personal health information and follow applicable privacy requirements.

## 11. Keep learning and reversibility visible
Prefer small, reviewable experiments when uncertainty is high. Record what changed, why, what evidence was expected, what happened, and whether the result supports or weakens the hypothesis.

## 12. Separate aspiration from delivery
A vision statement may describe a future possibility. A public product description must describe the current, verified offering. A roadmap, mock-up, or ambition must not silently become a present-tense promise.

## Default conflict order
Proposed order when principles conflict:
1. Prevent foreseeable user harm and preserve autonomy.
2. Maintain factual accuracy and privacy.
3. Preserve comprehension and trust.
4. Test usefulness and feasibility.
5. Optimize growth and efficiency.

This is proposed for owner review and does not replace professional legal, clinical, privacy, or regulatory advice.
