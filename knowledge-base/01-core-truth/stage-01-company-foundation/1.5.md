# 1.5 — Business Model Hypotheses & Operating Responsibilities

**Status:** Working draft — no business model or monetization approach confirmed

## Current status
The reviewed materials do not establish an approved pricing model, monetization mechanism, launch date, revenue target, or validated willingness to pay. Do not publish prices, free-trial terms, subscription promises, partnership claims, or revenue assumptions without confirmation and supporting evidence.

## Candidate models to evaluate later
These are investigation options, not approved plans:
- A free early-access or concept-validation experience.
- A consumer subscription for a verified set of ongoing organization features.
- A free core experience with optional paid capabilities.
- A family-oriented plan, if research supports shared household value and the permissions model is appropriate.
- An employer, provider, or institutional partnership, if a clear buyer, use case, evidence, and compliant operating model emerge.

Each option has distinct implications for trust, access, support, privacy, acquisition, and incentives. None should be chosen solely because it is common in adjacent products.

## Questions for each model
- Who experiences the problem, and who pays?
- Who receives value, and who controls the information?
- What recurring task would make continued use worthwhile?
- What do users currently spend in time, money, and effort?
- What would users expect to be free?
- What support and trust obligations would the model create?
- Could monetization incentives conflict with wellbeing or privacy?
- What evidence would disconfirm the model?

## Operating responsibilities
Until roles are formally assigned, treat these as responsibility areas rather than named owners:
- **Company strategy:** Maintain mission, strategic choices, and non-goals.
- **Product truth:** Verify availability and behavior before feature claims.
- **Research:** Maintain research plans, consent-aware notes, evidence summaries, and contradictory findings.
- **Brand and messaging:** Keep voice and visual use aligned with approved assets and current facts.
- **Marketing operations:** Keep campaigns reviewable, source-aware, and human-approved before publishing.
- **Trust and safety:** Escalate health claims, privacy concerns, potential harm, and uncertainty.
- **Measurement:** Define metrics and their interpretation limits before drawing conclusions.
- **Knowledge-base stewardship:** Track versions, sources, approvals, unresolved questions, and consistency.

One person may cover several responsibilities in an early-stage company. Every consequential decision should have a clear accountable human.

## Agent boundaries
Future AI agents may organize information, draft materials, identify contradictions, suggest experiments, and flag missing evidence. They must not silently approve their own health claims, invent evidence, change approved strategy, or treat generated answers as verified facts.

Agents should report what they did, which sources they used, what they inferred, what remains uncertain, and which human decision is needed.

## Sustainability
Potential costs may include research, product development, hosting, support, privacy/security work, content production, distribution, and professional review. Actual costs and unit economics are unknown and must not be fabricated. Growth plans should account for whether the company can responsibly support the expectations created by marketing.

## Owner decisions
- Which business models, if any, should be tested first?
- Who is accountable for product-truth approval, health-claim review, and final marketing approval?
- What support level can be promised during concept validation?
- Which financial and operating assumptions need evidence before a launch decision?
