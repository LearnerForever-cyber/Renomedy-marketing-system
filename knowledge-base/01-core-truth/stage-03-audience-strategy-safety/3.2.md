# 3.2 — Positioning, Value Proposition and Strategic Choices

**Status:** Draft strategy — owner approval pending

## Current public positioning evidence
The source register records the public headline “Care works better, together.” The current recorded concept concerns organizing everyday medicine-routine details and family context. The headline and concept description are evidence of public wording, not evidence that the proposition resonates or differentiates the product in the market.

## Working positioning hypothesis
For people and families trying to coordinate everyday medicine-routine information, Renomedy is a concept exploring ways to keep relevant details and family context easier to organize together.

This is a proposed positioning statement, not validated market truth. It avoids claiming that Renomedy improves adherence, prevents errors, provides medical advice, or offers specific live features.

## Strategic principles
- **Lead with the human task, not technical spectacle.** Describe the coordination problem in plain language.
- **Be transparent about stage.** Do not imply current availability while the latest recorded public status says in development / not yet available.
- **Prefer credible specificity over broad promises.** Say what is being explored; do not promise outcomes.
- **Treat trust as a requirement, not a slogan.** Health-related context needs careful handling, clear boundaries, and evidence.
- **Validate before scaling.** Do not choose channels or audiences based only on intuition.
- **Avoid fear-based marketing.** Do not use guilt, alarming medication-error scenarios, or pressure on caregivers to drive conversion.

## Alternatives and differentiation
The reviewed sources do not establish a verified competitor set, defensible differentiation, market size, or customer preference. Do not state that Renomedy is unique, first, best, or superior to named alternatives without current comparative research and substantiation.

Competitor and alternative research should include non-app workarounds such as paper notes, calendars, family messaging, and pharmacy/clinician processes as research categories, not as claims that all target users rely on them.

## Strategic choices requiring evidence
- Primary initial segment.
- Primary user job and scope for an initial release.
- Which language/region to prioritize.
- Which trust concerns are deal-breakers.
- Whether the first goal is interviews, concept testing, a waitlist, or another approved validation outcome.
- What evidence threshold must be met before changing positioning.
