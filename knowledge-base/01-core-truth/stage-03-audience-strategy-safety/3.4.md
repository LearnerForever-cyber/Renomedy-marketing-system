# 3.4 — Research Questions and Stage Exit Gate

**Status:** Working draft — owner approval pending

## Priority research questions
1. What exact task causes difficulty today, and how recently did it occur?
2. What do people currently do instead?
3. What are the practical and emotional costs of the current workaround?
4. Who is involved, and what consent or coordination friction exists?
5. What would make a medicine-related tool untrustworthy?
6. What would a participant need to see before considering a concept useful?
7. What would make them reject it?
8. What language, accessibility, device, or household constraints shape the task?
9. Which findings recur across independent participants, and which are isolated?
10. What evidence would contradict the leading segment or positioning hypothesis?

## Evidence discipline
- Record observed behaviour separately from participant opinion and researcher interpretation.
- Do not turn a small convenience sample into a market-size or prevalence claim.
- Do not report leading-question answers as unbiased validation.
- Preserve negative and contradictory evidence.
- Avoid collecting sensitive details that are not needed to answer the research question.
- Use participant quotations only with suitable consent and context.

## Stage exit checklist
- [ ] Owner approves the initial audience hypothesis.
- [ ] Research protocol, consent language, and data handling are approved.
- [ ] Interview or observation evidence is stored with provenance and limitations.
- [ ] Segment and problem statements are classified as supported, mixed, or unvalidated.
- [ ] Positioning language is tested rather than assumed.
- [ ] Safety and health-claim escalation process is accepted.
- [ ] Relevant evidence is reflected in the source register, fact ledger, and open-questions file.
