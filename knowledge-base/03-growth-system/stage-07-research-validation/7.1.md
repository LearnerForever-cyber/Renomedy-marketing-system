# 7.1 — Research Questions and Hypothesis Register

**Status:** Proposed research framework — owner approval pending

## Current hypotheses, not findings
- H1: Adult children coordinating a parent's medicine routine may experience a meaningful organization/coordination problem.
- H2: Keeping routine details and family context together may be useful to some households.
- H3: Trust, consent, accuracy, and privacy concerns may materially affect adoption of a medicine-related tool.
- H4: Some people may prefer a lightweight coordination aid over another complex health app.
- H5: The value proposition and channel fit may differ by caregiver role, age, language, and household context.

These are testable hypotheses derived from the concept and project direction. None is established as true by the reviewed evidence.

## Register fields
For each hypothesis, record ID, statement, segment, problem/task, current evidence, evidence status, disconfirming evidence, test method, success/failure criteria set before the test, owner, due date, result, limitations, and decision.

## Recommended question sequence
1. Ask about the last real occurrence of the task.
2. Ask what the person did and what happened next.
3. Ask which part was difficult, if any.
4. Ask what workaround they use and why.
5. Ask what they tried previously and why it did or did not work.
6. Only after understanding the experience, show a concept and ask for a reaction.
7. Ask what would make them reject it or distrust it.
8. Avoid asking leading questions such as “Would this app solve your problem?”

## Avoid false validation
Compliments, stated interest, social-media likes, survey intent, and hypothetical willingness to pay are weaker than observed behaviour or a meaningful commitment. Do not call a hypothesis validated merely because participants are polite or agree with a leading prompt.
