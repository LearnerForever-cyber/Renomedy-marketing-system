# 7.2 — Interview and Concept-Test Protocol

**Status:** Proposed protocol — owner approval pending

## Before sessions
- Define the decision the research must inform.
- Choose a relevant participant profile and document recruitment bias.
- Prepare neutral questions and a consistent session outline.
- Explain purpose, voluntary participation, note-taking, recording if any, and intended use.
- Obtain appropriate consent and use the minimum data needed.
- Avoid collecting prescriptions, diagnoses, medicine names, or other sensitive details unless essential and handled under an approved process.

## Session structure
1. Context and consent.
2. Recent real-world experience: “Tell me about the last time you had to organize or coordinate this.”
3. Existing workaround and trade-offs.
4. What was easy, difficult, uncertain, or costly?
5. Other people involved and permission/coordination considerations.
6. Concept reaction only after open exploration.
7. What feels confusing, untrustworthy, unnecessary, or risky?
8. What would the participant do next, and what would stop them?
9. Close, clarify data use, and offer withdrawal route if one has been promised.

## Notes format
Separate direct observations/quotes, participant interpretations, researcher interpretation, emerging themes, counterexamples, and follow-up questions. Record anonymized participant code, date, method, role, recruitment source, and consent status. Do not invent quotes or fill missing notes from memory.

## Concept-test cautions
- Label screens as illustrative concepts when they are not live.
- Do not simulate features as if they already work.
- Do not promise future release or ask for sensitive information unnecessarily.
- A stated preference is not proof of adoption or payment.

## Ethics and safety
Pause a session if it becomes a request for medical advice. Do not provide diagnosis, dose guidance, or treatment recommendations. Direct the person to an appropriate healthcare professional for medical decisions.
