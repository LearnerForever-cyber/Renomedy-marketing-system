# 9.2 — Experiment Analysis and Decision Rules

**Status:** Proposed analysis framework — owner approval pending

## Before an experiment
Record the hypothesis, audience, intervention, comparison if any, primary outcome, guardrail metrics, duration or stopping rule, sample/recruitment plan, known confounders, and decision that will follow. Do not choose success criteria after seeing results.

## Interpretation
- Separate observed results from explanations.
- Include sample size, missingness, recruitment bias, and uncertainty.
- Avoid causal claims from uncontrolled observations.
- Do not generalize a small qualitative sample to the whole market.
- Keep negative and ambiguous outcomes.
- Do not repeatedly check results and stop only when they look favorable without a predeclared rule.

## Decision outcomes
- Continue or repeat if the evidence is promising but fragile.
- Refine the message or concept if a specific confusion is observed.
- Stop if the hypothesis is contradicted, the test is unsafe, or the cost exceeds the learning value.
- Mark inconclusive if data quality or sample is inadequate.

## Optimization safeguards
A short-term increase in clicks is not automatically a win if it comes from misleading claims, fear, poor-quality participants, or a broken promise. Include safety, comprehension, and quality guardrails alongside growth metrics.

## Current status
No completed experiments or performance datasets were found in the reviewed evidence. No optimization conclusion should be asserted until an experiment record exists.
