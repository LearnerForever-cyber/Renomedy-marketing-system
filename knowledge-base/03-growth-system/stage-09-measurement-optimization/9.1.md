# 9.1 — Metric Dictionary and Evidence Requirements

**Status:** Proposed metric dictionary — owner approval pending

## Metric principles
A metric is useful only when its definition, data source, collection method, time window, denominator, and limitations are known. Do not report a number without explaining what was counted and how.

## Candidate metrics by stage
- **Research:** eligible participants contacted; sessions completed; recruitment source; no-show rate; participant-role mix.
- **Concept comprehension:** proportion of tested participants who can accurately explain current status and concept after exposure. Define the scoring rubric before testing.
- **Research conversion:** completed approved feedback sessions divided by eligible invitations, with exclusions documented.
- **Content engagement:** reach, views, clicks, or saves only where platform analytics provide the metric; these are not proof of need or purchase intent.
- **Landing-page behaviour:** visits, CTA clicks, and completed actions only if instrumentation and consent/privacy handling are verified.
- **Product usage and retention:** do not report until a real product, event definitions, and valid measurement exist.

These are candidate metrics, not current Renomedy results or confirmed instrumentation.

## Metric record fields
Name, question answered, precise formula, numerator, denominator, source, event definition, time window, segmentation, exclusions, collection/consent basis, owner, known biases, and decision it can inform.

## Guard against vanity metrics
Impressions, followers, likes, and raw traffic can indicate distribution but do not by themselves validate a problem, product value, retention, or business viability. Pair each metric with the decision it is meant to support.

## Current baseline
No verified baseline was present in the reviewed source set. Record “Not measured” rather than 0; zero means the metric was measured and no qualifying events occurred, while not measured means no valid observation is available.
