# 8.2 — Content Pillars and Campaign Rules

**Status:** Proposed content system — owner approval pending

## Candidate content pillars
1. **Everyday coordination:** explore how people organize routine details without claiming universal pain.
2. **Caregiver perspective:** discuss coordination respectfully and avoid portraying older adults as incapable.
3. **Trust and clarity:** explain what a concept is and is not; avoid medical advice.
4. **Building transparently:** share approved progress only when facts and status are current.
5. **Research invitation:** invite relevant people to share experiences only when a real, approved destination exists.

These are candidate pillars, not evidence that the audience wants them or that any channel is active.

## Campaign brief minimum
Every campaign should document objective, audience hypothesis, insight/source, core message, exact claims, CTA and tested destination, channel, content owner, review requirements, success measure, budget/time assumptions, dates, and post-campaign decision.

## Content safety rules
- Do not imply the app is available when the latest verified status does not support it.
- Do not use fear, shame, or guilt around medication or caregiving.
- Do not publish clinical claims, unsupported feature descriptions, fake testimonials, or invented statistics.
- Clearly label illustrative screens and future concepts.
- Do not collect sensitive health data through casual social engagement.
- Use appropriate review for translated health-related wording.

## Repurposing rule
A claim approved in one context is not automatically safe in every context. Recheck whether a shorter caption, visual, headline, or translation changes the implied meaning.
