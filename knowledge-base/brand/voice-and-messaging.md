# Voice and Messaging

## Voice derived from the current public website

- Calm, thoughtful, warm and human.
- Plain-language and non-alarmist.
- Family-centered without guilt or blame.
- Clear about being a concept in development.
- Respectful of healthcare professionals and medical boundaries.

## Publicly used lines (short reference examples)

- “Care works better, together.”
- “Small details. Shared responsibility.”
- “Less piecing it together. More being there.”
- “Here for the routine. Not the medical decisions.”

These lines are examples from the current public site, not proof that every line must be used in all contexts.

## Messaging rules

1. Focus on everyday routine details and family context.
2. Present the product as something being explored/developed, not a released app.
3. Explain the concept without promising a result.
4. Avoid guilt, panic, fear-based health messaging and ageist stereotypes.
5. Do not portray older adults as universally confused, helpless or technologically incapable.
6. Do not use medical jargon where plain language works.
7. Keep product claims tied to the source register and current development status.
8. Do not imply that a prototype screen is a functioning product feature.

## Safer examples

- “Renomedy is exploring a more considered way for families to keep everyday medicine-routine details together.”
- “A concept for bringing routine details and family context into one place.”
- “Explore the idea behind Renomedy.”

Use a CTA only if it points to a working destination and matches the current site.

## Avoid

- “Download Renomedy now.”
- “Never miss a dose again.”
- “Guaranteed medication safety.”
- “Reads every prescription perfectly.”
- “Predicts exactly when medicines will run out.”
- “Clinically proven,” “certified,” or “100% secure” without explicit supporting evidence and owner approval.
